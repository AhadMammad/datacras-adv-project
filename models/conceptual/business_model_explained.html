How the agency's business runs, system by system

An advertising agency wins a client, plans a campaign, produces the creative, buys media, serves ads, measures the results and settles the money. Each step is recorded in one of six operational databases, and all six feed the analytics lakehouse.

The life of a campaign

Work moves left to right. The colour of each step shows the system that records it. Media ops appears twice: it holds the plan and, later, the buy.

Who owns what, and who reads it

Each business concept is mastered in exactly one system. Other systems store only its identifier, as a soft reference with no database constraint across the boundary. Each arrow points from the system that owns the data to the system that uses it.

Anatomy of a campaign

The middle column is the chain every report rolls up through, from the client paying for the campaign down to a single conversion. The boxes at the sides are what each level gets from elsewhere.

How the money is controlled

Spend is authorised twice. The client approves it in a client insertion order under their agreement, and the agency commits it to a media vendor in a media insertion order. The vendor IO carries out the client IO.

Before a supplier is paid, the ERP checks three independent numbers against each other: what was authorised, what the supplier invoiced, and what the ad server actually delivered.

From client brief to a live ad

Creative work moves from the client's request, through production and proofing in work management, to an approved asset that media ops traffics as an ad.

Where the analytics come from

None of the six systems is the reporting layer. Change data capture streams every insert and update into the Databricks lakehouse, where the systems come together in the Advertising MVM.

Rules worth remembering

  • Every campaign belongs to one advertiser and runs in at least one flight.
  • A line item sits in exactly one flight and can serve many ads.
  • A supplier is recorded once, in media ops. The ERP only refers to it.
  • Audience segments are mastered in the CDP and targeted by line items.
  • Conversions are credited to delivery events under the advertiser's attribution model.
  • Links inside one database are enforced foreign keys. Links between databases are soft references.
